PROJECT 04 / LOW-LEVEL PROGRAMMING

ProjectPP.

A slideshow tool for NES hardware, made with four friends in 6502 assembly. My first time writing assembly, and the place where I learned to make a Nintendo beep.

01

My contribution

For most of us, this was our first time writing assembly, and it took me a while to get comfortable with it. Early on, I focused on testing, learning the codebase and quality-of-life improvements: fixing the order of actions in the main loop, and supporting slide numbers up to three digits (who would ever need 999 slides?).

Then I got entrusted with a feature of my own: sound.

02

Making a NES beep

The problem. When you move between slides, the console should give a short beep: high going forward, low going back. Simple to describe. On a NES, a bit less so.

What I considered. First, an existing precompiled NES audio library. In theory, the easy way. In practice, the sounds coming out were not the ones I’d made in the editor, and nobody, not even our professor, could figure out why.

What I chose. “Fall seven times, stand up eight,” I told myself. I wasn’t going to let a library bug stop our team, so I went from forum to forum to see how other developers program NES sound, and wrote it myself. It uses only the first pulse (square wave) channel:

  • $4000 sets a 25% duty cycle at constant volume 10.
  • $4001 turns the sweep off, and $4003 sets a long length counter.
  • $4002 sets the pitch: a period of 100 for the high beep (~1.1 kHz) and 180 for the low one (~620 Hz).
  • A one-byte timer in zero page is set to 30 frames and counted down in the NMI, once per frame. When it hits zero, the volume goes back to 0. At 60 fps, that’s half a second.

The beeps also respect the tool’s sound on/off setting.

The result. A “beep” going forward and a “boop” going back. It all comes down to knowing which bit to set, at which address, at exactly what time and for how long. Along the way, I learned how sound is made digitally at the very lowest level.

03

Looking back

I wish I’d studied up a bit more in the early stages. With a stronger start, I could have taken on something bigger, like an in-app text editor. But I’m proud of what I made, and of not giving up on it.
